Public Opinion on the War Against Iran

By Ishrat Tuli & Bradley Benjamin · March 2026 · 5 min read
✦

On Feb. 28th, following weeks of military buildup and threats from President Donald Trump, the United States and Israel launched massive strikes at Iran. President Trump stated that his goals were to eliminate Iran’s nuclear and missile programs and to transform the country’s leadership.

· · ·

The U.S. and Iran have had rapidly escalating tensions since the Islamic Revolution in 1979, following the toppling of a steadfast American ally Shah Mohammed Reza Pahlavi. Following Hamas’, an Iranian ally’s, attack on Israel in Oct. 2023, American and Israeli forces bombed Tehran, Iran’s capital, killing at least 20 senior military commanders. The Trump Administration initially sought negotiations, until ultimately settling for war.

Protests against the war erupted worldwide almost immediately, most notable in major U.S. cities. Demonstrators in New York City held an emergency protest in Times Square on Feb. 28th following the U.S. Government’s announcement.

“...they just could have come to an agreement, ” Said Emily J, a liberal arts student at NYU, when asked if she believes that the U.S. made enough of a diplomatic effort with Iran before using military force or not, “Just like, it's just gonna be a war on innocent people more than just all over oil…”

After unrelenting military strikes, President Trump posted on Truth Social on Feb. 28th that Iranian supreme leader Ayatollah Khamenei was killed. Iran unleashed attacks on U.S. and allied military bases across the Middle East. As of March 17th, 13 U.S. service members have been killed with approximately 200 wounded.

“I'm very concerned about the amount of people that are going to die during service. I mean, I have a friend that's in the Navy and it's kind of scary to think he could be just, you know, sent off any day.” Yasmin M., a New York native said, “... and they do it so that they could have free schooling and they could have the benefits. and you know like they could turn a new leaf and obviously they knew the risk of joining but it's just crazy to think that like they're going to be serving and it's like a cause."

“There's just been nothing but false promises. He's doing the exact opposite of what he promised to do and we're funding it.”
— Said Yasmin.

Beyond fears about diplomacy and troop deployment, other New Yorkers interviewed have expressed concern that ordinary people are absorbing the cost of decisions made by those in power.

“It’s been a lot of pointless mass destruction for no reason, especially with something that has such a high margin of error. It’s civilians [dying],” said Gordon, 25, an office supervisor in midtown.

Civilian casualties have also intensified criticism of the War. AP reported that a preliminary U.S. military investigation found outdated intelligence likely led to the strike on the Iranian elementary school that killed more than 165 people, many of them children.

That sense of frustration regarding the war seems to have amplified already existing tensions among communities divided within the city.

“There is this certain air in New York. Especially around anti-Semitism and the reason behind the war. Our government is making certain decisions and everyone here comes from all types of different places. So that worries me, ‘cause I feel like as a city, we’re not really united,” said Marcus, a Data Analyst.

As the conflict continues, New Yorkers interviewed described concerns over the war being justified as necessity while its human and financial costs are carried by the common people.

“It’s affected me in the tax money I’m paying. Knowing that it’s being utilized for things that don’t concern us. Instead of helping communities, helping the country as a whole,” Gordon said.

Marcus questioned the broader logic behind continued U.S. military intervention.

“Someone has convinced America that we can only progress through war and it’s a pretty outdated belief. We’re draining out Earth’s resources by fighting or spending so much of our budget,”
— Marcus Said.
